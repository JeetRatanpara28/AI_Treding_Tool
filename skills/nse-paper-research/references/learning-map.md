# Trading research coverage map

This foundation-to-advanced map is not a claim that every market rule is mastered.
Operational rules and events require repeated verification. Reading a syllabus is
not completing its paid course. Demonstrate the capability, then expose its limits.

| Topic | Capability to demonstrate |
| --- | --- |
| 1. Instruments and horizon | Distinguish equity, ETFs, indices and derivatives; keep implementation cash-equity paper-only. |
| 2. Intraday, swing, momentum and investing | State holding horizon and exits; do not mix strategies' evidence. |
| 3. OHLCV and timeframes | Explain bar contents and the internal price path they do not reveal. |
| 4. Sessions and calendars | Use verified trading dates, special sessions, timezone and finalization cutoff. |
| 5. Orders and market mechanics | Explain conceptual market/limit/stop behavior and non-fill without broker access. |
| 6. Liquidity and execution | Account for spreads, slippage, gaps, turnover, tick/lot sizes and price bands. |
| 7. Corporate actions | Distinguish announcement/ex/record dates and price/share-count effects. |
| 8. Trends and regimes | Distinguish trend, range and volatility using past information only. |
| 9. Support, resistance and pivots | Define levels mechanically with confirmation delay. |
| 10. Candlesticks | Explain geometry/context without claiming universal buying probabilities. |
| 11. Multi-bar chart patterns | Supply reproducible conditions or mark the interpretation subjective. |
| 12. Indicators | Separate trend, momentum, volume and volatility; avoid double-counting correlated signals. |
| 13. Entry hypotheses | Define a completed-bar trigger and earliest eligible costed fill. |
| 14. Exit hypotheses | Specify invalidation, stop, profit/time exit and gap handling before entry. |
| 15. Position sizing | Reconcile loss budget, cash, integer shares, lot size and costs. |
| 16. Portfolio risk | Track sleeve budgets, correlation, concentration, heat and drawdown. |
| 17. Costs and taxes | Version fees and verify primary sources; separate fees from investor-specific income tax. |
| 18. Fundamentals | Compare revenue, margins, debt, cash flow, dilution, valuation and accounting basis. |
| 19. Governance/event risk | Review audit issues, promoter disclosures, pledges and litigation with uncertainty. |
| 20. Macro and sectors | Form hypotheses about rates, inflation, currencies and commodities without deterministic calls. |
| 21. News workflow | Verify original filings, public availability, versions, identities and duplicate stories. |
| 22. Data engineering | Check source, schema, dates, missingness, symbols, revisions and adjusted/raw basis. |
| 23. Research bias | Identify look-ahead, survivorship, selection, data snooping and reused holdouts. |
| 24. Performance/benchmarks | Evaluate net returns, drawdown, uncertainty, exposure, turnover and matched benchmarks. |
| 25. Psychology | Detect FOMO, revenge trades, hindsight stories and arbitrary risk escalation. |
| 26. AI/ML and monitoring | Establish a baseline, use available training data only and monitor drift without automatic deployment. |

Use the linked operational references in `SKILL.md`. Keep statuses distinct:
explained, source-checked, specified, implemented, unit-tested, empirically tested,
independently replicated and approved. An explanation or unit test is not evidence
of a profitable trading edge.
